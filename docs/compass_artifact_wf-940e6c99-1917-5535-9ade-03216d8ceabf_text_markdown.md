# Client Acquisition and Signal-Based Origination Strategy for Meritus Via

## TL;DR
- The single highest-leverage move is to build a proprietary "dispute propensity" signal engine that fuses free UK statutory data (Companies House API, The Gazette insolvency feed, TCC cause lists and Find Case Law, Find a Tender/Contracts Finder OCDS feeds, the Payment Practices register, and Building Safety Regulator Gateway data) into a scored, named weekly watchlist of companies and projects heading into dispute; the founder's software capability makes this a genuine and defensible edge that none of HKA, Ankura, Secretariat or Diales exposes as a client-facing asset.
- The three richest near-term origination channels are (1) litigation funders and ATE insurers, where becoming the default quantum/merits vetting partner puts Meritus Via upstream of every other adviser; (2) insolvency practitioners inheriting distressed contract "books of claims" (post-ISG, and post-Bresco v Lonsdale adjudication is now available to companies in liquidation); and (3) the Building Safety Act white space, where Gateway 2 delay quantum and Remediation Contribution Order/Building Liability Order disputes are quantifiable, named in public tribunal decisions and government registers, and branded by nobody.
- The most reliable calendar-driven origination method exploits limitation and retention timing: disputes cluster 12 to 24 months after practical completion, and the Defective Premises Act 1972 retrospective 30-year window (via s.135 Building Safety Act 2022) plus the 15-year prospective window create a datable cliff-edge of claims; combine this with sector programme timing (AMP8's £104bn, CP7, RIS3, HS2, Sizewell C) to predict where delay and quantum work will land in 2026 to 2030.

## Key Findings

**1. Most early dispute evidence is in free or cheap public data, and most of it has an API.** Companies House, The Gazette, Find a Tender/Contracts Finder, Find Case Law, the Payment Practices register and the BSR all publish machine-readable data under the Open Government Licence or equivalent. The competitive edge is not access; it is fusion, scoring and speed. A competent developer can assemble a materially better origination signal than a partner reading Construction Enquirer over coffee.

**2. The signals differ sharply in lead time and false-positive rate.** Employment adverts and listed-company write-downs give the longest lead (6 to 18 months) but need interpretation; winding-up petitions and adjudication enforcement judgments are near-certain dispute markers but arrive late (the dispute already exists). The value is in combining a long-lead, noisy signal with a short-lead, precise one to time the approach.

**3. The BSA regime is a structurally under-served, publicly-named pipeline.** The Developer Remediation Contract signatory list, the Responsible Actors Scheme, the monthly Building Safety Remediation data release, and First-tier/Upper Tribunal and Court of Appeal decisions (Triathlon Homes v Stratford Village) name the exact parties in dispute. In August 2025, in its first twelve months of Gateway 2 operation, the BSR's own transparency data recorded a new-build higher-risk-building median approval time of 43 weeks with an approval rate of just 39%, against a statutory decision period of 12 weeks; that gap creates a quantifiable, brandable delay claim between developers and contractors that no competitor markets explicitly.

**4. Boutique conflict-of-interest exploitation is systematic, not opportunistic.** HKA, Ankura, Secretariat and the QS-firm disputes arms are conflicted out of a large share of instructions because they act for so many parties across a small market. A 10 to 12 person partner-led firm with a clean conflict check is the natural overflow beneficiary, if it is visible to the referrers at the moment the conflict bites.

## Details

### Part A. The dispute-signal data sources, and exactly how to mine each

#### A1. Companies House (UK)
- **What it is and URL:** Free REST API returning JSON for company profile, officers, filing history, charges, insolvency and persons with significant control. Developer hub at developer.company-information.service.gov.uk; specification at developer-specs.company-information.service.gov.uk. Register for a free API key.
- **Access model:** Free, including commercial use. Rate limit 600 requests per 5 minutes per key (2/second), HTTP 429 with Retry-After if exceeded; a higher limit can be requested. There is also a streaming API (filing history, insolvency, charges) and free bulk snapshots of basic company data for the initial load, since a full one-request-per-company sweep of ~5 million companies takes about a month.
- **Signals to mine and why they matter:**
  - *Late filing of accounts / change of accounting reference date:* classic cash-stress and audit-dispute signal. Lead time long (6 to 12 months), false-positive rate moderate.
  - *Auditor resignation:* high-value distress signal, low false-positive.
  - *Charges registered/satisfied (/company/{number}/charges):* a new floating charge or refinancing often precedes contract distress; satisfaction of charges can signal a sale or restructure. Moderate lead, moderate noise.
  - *Director resignations, especially a commercial or finance director departure:* correlates with a distressed final account. Combine with LinkedIn.
  - *Group restructures, filleted/small-company accounts:* limited visibility for the SME majority (no P&L until ECCTA reforms bite), so treat absence of data as its own flag on SPV-heavy developer structures.
- **How to build it:** Maintain a local mirror updated via the streaming API (the pattern used by AML vendors); alert on filing-history categories and charge events for a watchlist of contractors, subcontractors and developer SPVs.

#### A2. The Gazette (London/Edinburgh/Belfast) and Insolvency Service
- **What it is and URL:** thegazette.co.uk is the UK's official public record of insolvency. Notice types include petitions to wind up companies (notice 2450), petitions to wind up partnerships (2451), creditors' voluntary winding-up resolutions (2441), meetings of creditors (2442), appointment of liquidators (2443) and notices to creditors (2446).
- **Access model:** The Gazette offers an XML submission schema and developer documentation, plus a paid **Gazette Data Service** for structured company/insolvency/deceased-estate data feeds designed for "the earliest indicator of business distress". Free search and free per-notice viewing are available; the bulk/real-time data feed is the commercial product. Insolvency Service monthly statistics are published on GOV.UK.
- **Signals and lead time:** A winding-up petition against a subcontractor is a near-certain marker that its accounts with main contractors are about to be contested (retention, final account, set-off). Lead time is short: the dispute is imminent or live. Crucially, a **subcontractor insolvency is a leading indicator for the main contractor's account**: the main contractor faces completion cost claims, defective work set-off and delay, and the liquidator will pursue the sub's unpaid final account. Cross-reference each petition against the watchlist of live projects (from A4) to find the affected main contractor.
- **The Companies Court daily list** (Chancery Division) shows upcoming petition hearings before Gazette advertisement, giving a few days' extra lead.

#### A3. Court listings and judgments (TCC, Find Case Law, BAILII)
- **TCC daily cause list:** published by HMCTS via the Court and Tribunal Hearings service (court-tribunal-hearings.service.gov.uk), typically by 4pm the day before, under the Open Government Licence v3.0. It names parties, case references, judges and courtrooms. The TCC sits in the Rolls Building, 7 Rolls Buildings, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL. Third-party mirror CauseAlert already offers free name alerts on TCC listings, which is worth monitoring and also a competitive warning that this signal is not secret.
- **Why it matters:** the TCC list is dominated by adjudication enforcement (Part 7 and Part 8) and arbitration claims. A party appearing on the list is, by definition, in a live dispute and has already instructed lawyers and usually experts. An adjudication enforcement judgment is effectively a public directory of who is in dispute, who their solicitors and counsel are, and often which expert firm acted. This is the cleanest possible referral-mapping and displacement dataset.
- **Find Case Law (National Archives):** caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk, judgments since 2001, with a public API and Atom feed. **Licensing is the critical constraint:** ordinary reuse is under the **Open Justice Licence**, which explicitly **does not permit computational analysis**. Any programmatic bulk searching, extraction or enrichment requires applying for a separate **computational analysis licence** (no application charge). Terms warn that compiling an index in violation may lead to blocking. So: build the case-law monitor only under a granted computational-analysis licence; this is a genuine compliance gate the firm must clear before scraping. BAILII remains a supplementary free source (and covers Scotland, Northern Ireland and Ireland).
- **Method:** monitor new TCC and Court of Appeal construction judgments; extract party names, adviser names and expert names; feed into both a displacement pipeline (who lost, who might switch adviser) and a conflict-map (which competitor acted, hence is now conflicted for the counterparty).

#### A4. Public procurement data (Find a Tender, Contracts Finder, devolved portals)
- **URLs and APIs:** Find a Tender (find-tender.service.gov.uk) and Contracts Finder (contractsfinder.service.gov.uk) both publish notices in **Open Contracting Data Standard (OCDS) 1.1.5 JSON via free APIs** under the Open Government Licence, with commercial reuse permitted. Find a Tender release-package endpoint: find-tender.service.gov.uk/api/1.0/ocdsReleasePackages (max 100 releases/page, cursor pagination via links.next, updatedFrom/updatedTo filters). Devolved equivalents: Sell2Wales, Public Contracts Scotland, eTendersNI.
- **What to mine:** contract award notices give buyer, supplier, value, CPV code, contract period start/end and framework. Extract contract **duration and value** to compute an overrun-risk score (long-duration, high-value, fixed-price civils on stretched programmes score highest). Known data-quality issues: duplicate supplier identifiers, some empty supplier-name and unrealistic date fields, so deduplicate and validate.
- **Procurement Act 2023 transparency notices (the new and most valuable layer):** in force 24 February 2025 via the enhanced Find a Tender and Central Digital Platform. For contracts over £5 million, authorities must set at least three KPIs (s.52) and publish a **Contract Performance Notice at least every 12 months and on termination** (s.71) reporting performance against those KPIs. This publishes **poor supplier performance, breach and termination data** for the first time. There is also a centralised **debarment list** (mandatory and discretionary) naming excluded suppliers. A contractor receiving a poor Contract Performance Notice, or facing exclusion, is a named, timed origination target. Build a monitor for CPNs and debarment entries.

#### A5. Payment Practices and Performance Reporting
- **URL and access:** check-payment-practices.service.gov.uk, free, searchable, no API but scrapable per-report pages. Large companies (meeting two of: turnover £54m, balance sheet £27m, 250 employees) must report twice a year: average days to pay, percentage paid within 30/31-60/61+ days, percentage paid late, and (from 1 January 2025 amendments) retention practices for qualifying construction contracts. EY compiles sector aggregates.
- **Signals:** a contractor's **deteriorating average days-to-pay across successive reporting periods** is a direct cash-stress marker that precedes supplier disputes and its own final-account battles. Build UK also benchmarks Tier 1 members' payment performance. Cross-reference slow-and-worsening payers against the winding-up-petition feed among their subcontractors.

#### A6. Building Safety Regulator Gateway data (the white-space quantifier)
- **URLs:** BSR building control approval application data is published on GOV.UK and buildingsafety.campaign.gov.uk. The data covers Gateway 2 (and now early Gateway 3) determinations, approval rates and median approval times.
- **The quantifiable claim:** BSR Gateway 2 has run far beyond the statutory decision period. In August 2025, at the twelve-month mark of the Innovation Unit's operation, the BSR's own data showed a new-build HRB **median approval time of 43 weeks with a 39% approval rate**, against a **12-week statutory decision period**. By the 12 weeks to 30 May 2026, medians had improved to 22 weeks (new-build Innovation Unit) and 34 to 39 weeks (external remediation), with **1,445 live Gateway 2 cases holding 38,775 residential units at 30 May 2026**. Earlier BSR figures showed roughly 50% of applications for existing higher-risk buildings and 45% for new HRBs rejected up to March 2025.
- **Why it is origination gold:** every week a project sits in Gateway 2 beyond programme is prolongation cost and, on many contracts, a compensable or contested delay event between developer and main contractor. This is a discrete, repeatable, quantifiable claim type that no competitor currently brands explicitly. The BSR data itself sizes the addressable market (thousands of buildings, tens of thousands of units). Productise as a "Gateway Delay Quantum" offering.

#### A7. BSA remediation registers and funds
- **Developer Remediation Contract signatories:** named list on GOV.UK. **39 developers signed by the 13 March 2023 deadline** (announced 14 March 2023 by Michael Gove), rising to **48 by 18 May 2023** and over 50 subsequently, including Barratt Developments, Bellway, Berkeley-scale housebuilders and named entities such as Ballymore, Canary Wharf Group, Crest Nicholson, Galliard, Grosvenor, Keepmoat, Land Securities, Lendlease and McCarthy & Stone; the contract covers 11m+ buildings developed over the 30 years to 4 April 2022 and the signed agreements were expected to raise at least £2 billion. Non-signatories and RAS-eligible refuseniks face prohibitions on development and building-control approval.
- **Building Safety Remediation monthly data release (GOV.UK):** updated monthly (developer-contract progress quarterly). As at end June 2026, 4,469 buildings with unsafe cladding were being monitored, of which 1,681 (38%) had completed remediation. Building Safety Fund: of 678 high-rise buildings proceeding, 470 (69%) completed at end April 2026. Cladding Safety Scheme: 1,325 buildings assessed eligible at end May 2026. A per-developer progress chart is published.
- **Remediation Acceleration Plan (published 2 December 2024, gov.uk/government/collections/remediation-acceleration-plan):** targets all 18m+ government-funded buildings remediated by end 2029; every 11m+ building remediated or with a completion date or enforcement by end 2029; a Building Safety Levy to raise ~£3.4 billion; a forthcoming Remediation Bill creating a legal duty to remediate.
- **Tribunal decisions naming parties:** Remediation Contribution Orders (s.124 BSA) and Building Liability Orders are decided in the First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) and Upper Tribunal (Lands Chamber), published on the judiciary and tribunal decision databases. In **Triathlon Homes LLP v Stratford Village Development Partnership, Get Living plc and East Village Management Limited** ([2024] UKFTT 26 (PC), upheld [2025] EWCA Civ 846), the FTT ordered **Get Living plc and SVDP to pay £17.947 million** towards remedial costs (total remediation cost exceeding £24.5m, with £27.5m of Building Safety Fund grant in play), and the decision names every party, adviser and counsel. Each such decision is a named longlist of parties who are in, or adjacent to, live BSA disputes.

#### A8. Sector capital programmes generating 2026 to 2030 disputes
- **AMP8 water (April 2025 to March 2030):** Ofwat's December 2024 PR24 final determination approved ~£104bn (roughly double AMP7's ~£51bn), confirmed by the CMA on 26 March 2026 (five appellants, Anglian, Northumbrian, South East, Southern, Wessex, gained £463m, 17% of what they sought; envelope unchanged). Industry is widely reported as behind: Galliford Try's environment MD said at the British Water conference in November 2025 the sector was already falling behind; Anglian had spent £1.1bn of an £11bn plan; end dates slipping to 2032-2033. A quadrupling of enhancement spend into a resource-constrained supply chain on NEC contracts is a near-guaranteed delay/quantum wave. Tier 1s include Costain, Galliford Try, MWH Treatment, Kier, Balfour Beatty, Mott MacDonald Bentley, Barhale, Stonbury and the various @one/water-company alliances.
- **National Highways RIS3 (2025-2030), Network Rail CP7 (2024-2029), HS2, Lower Thames Crossing, Sizewell C, SMRs, data centres, gigafactories, and Ofgem ASTI/RIIO-T3 grid programmes:** each is large, long and contractually complex. Balfour Beatty's order book now includes ~£3bn of Sizewell C work (Programme Alliance with Laing O'Rourke and Bouygues); National Grid proposed up to £35bn of transmission investment 2026-2031; the East Coast Cluster/Net Zero Teesside carbon capture programmes are live (Costain). Grid connection and transmission (ASTI) is the fastest-growing dispute frontier.

#### A9. Listed-contractor RNS and accounts signals
- **What to mine:** RNS announcements (via London Stock Exchange/investor relations), annual reports and half-year results for **contract write-downs, onerous contract provisions and exceptional items**. A written-down contract in an annual report is effectively a public advertisement of a live dispute. Balfour Beatty's FY2025 results noted net non-underlying charges of £70m after tax for increased UK Building Safety Act provisions and an adverse US Highways jury verdict, partly offset by £80m insurance recoveries and £30m warranty release credits, and a cost overrun at a US Civils project. Monitor Balfour Beatty, Galliford Try, Kier, Morgan Sindall, Costain, Renew Holdings, Keller and the mid-tier.
- **Method:** scrape RNS feeds and PDF accounts; NLP-flag phrases such as "onerous contract", "contract provision", "loss-making contract", "adverse adjudication", "exceptional item", "legacy contract". Long lead (results are periodic) but very low false-positive.

#### A10. Employment/hiring signals
- **What to mine:** job adverts for commercial managers, quantity surveyors with "claims"/"final account" briefs, contract managers and delay analysts at a specific contractor or named project indicate a distressed final account or a claims build-up. The departure of a commercial director (A1 cross-reference) is a strong marker. Longest lead of all (6 to 18 months), highest noise, so use as an amplifier not a trigger.

#### A11. Insurance, credit and construction-data market signals
- **Insurance first responders:** latent defects (structural warranty) insurers, professional indemnity insurers of design consultants, contractors' all-risks insurers, and the delegated-authority/coverholder ecosystem at Lloyd's; loss adjusters and forensic accountants receive claims before advisers do. These are referral sources, not scrape targets.
- **Credit and trade-credit data:** Creditsafe, Experian, Red Flag Alert (build-up distress scoring) and trade-credit insurers' appetite changes flag subcontractor stress. Construction-specific: Glenigan and Barbour ABI (quote-priced annual subscriptions, roughly £5,000 to £10,000+/year, project-level data through planning to contract and subcontract award, with Barbour phone-confirming start/finish dates on 92%+ of live projects at award); Builders' Conference (member tender/award data, strong in London and the South East); The Construction Index and Construction Enquirer (free news, contract leagues, real-time award and insolvency signals). Glenigan/Barbour are the mineable databases for an overrun watchlist; the news sites are free real-time monitors.

### Part B. The signal engine architecture

- **Stack fit:** the founder's React/Python/Docker/AWS/OpenSearch/RAG stack is exactly right. Ingest each source into OpenSearch; normalise to a common entity model keyed on Companies House number; run a nightly scoring job; surface a React dashboard with a ranked weekly watchlist and alerting.
- **API vs scrape vs licence:**
  - *Clean APIs (free, commercial reuse OK):* Companies House, Find a Tender, Contracts Finder (OCDS), TCC cause lists (OGL v3.0).
  - *Licence-gated:* Find Case Law requires a computational-analysis licence for any bulk/programmatic use (Open Justice Licence forbids it). Apply before building the case-law monitor.
  - *Commercial feeds:* The Gazette Data Service (paid) for real-time insolvency; Glenigan/Barbour under subscription terms that typically restrict redistribution, so use internally only.
  - *Scrape with care:* Payment Practices register (no API), RNS/investor pages, job boards, planning portals, BSR pages. Respect robots.txt and terms; several sites (CauseAlert on listings) show reuse is feasible under OGL.
- **Scoring model ("dispute propensity"):** weight signals by lead time and precision. Illustrative: winding-up petition against a subcontractor on a watchlisted project (+high, short lead); adjudication enforcement in TCC list (+very high, live); adverse Contract Performance Notice/debarment (+high); deteriorating payment days across two periods (+medium); auditor resignation or FD departure (+medium); contract write-down in accounts (+high, low noise); Gateway 2 case beyond median (+high for BSA pillar); hiring of claims QS (+low, amplifier). Decay scores over time and require at least two independent signals before a partner-contact recommendation, to control false positives.
- **Is the engine a product?** Yes, in two ways. As a **business-development asset** it is decisive. As a **reciprocity tool**, a curated, non-competitive intelligence feed (for example a fortnightly "distress digest" of public-record events) given free to a small set of construction solicitors and funders creates obligation and keeps Meritus Via top-of-mind at the instruction moment. A fuller commercial data product overlaps with VeriCase's Lifecycle Intelligence Platform and should be sequenced under that brand, not diluted. Do not sell raw Companies House/Gazette data (against spirit and sometimes terms); sell the analysis and the judgement.

### Part C. Non-obvious channel and referral architecture

- **Litigation funders and ATE insurers (the upstream position):** Burford, Harbour, Therium, Bench Walk, Omni Bridgeway, Augusta, Litigation Capital Management, plus ATE/legal-expenses insurers Litica and Temple Legal Protection. Funders make a fund/no-fund decision that requires independent merits and quantum sizing before any other adviser is locked in. Becoming a funder's default construction vetting partner puts Meritus Via upstream of the solicitor and the expert appointment. Concrete play: offer funders a fixed-fee 5-day "merits and quantum triage" on construction matters, and position the VeriCase delay engine as the tool that de-risks their quantum assumption. Note the post-PACCAR/Therium v Bugsby funding-enforceability uncertainty is live, so pitch on decision-quality, not volume.
- **Insolvency practitioners and restructuring teams (the "book of claims"):** Begbies Traynor, Interpath, Teneo, Quantuma, FRP, Kroll, EY (ISG administrator). When a contractor enters administration it holds a portfolio of unresolved final accounts and claims that the administrator must value and monetise urgently. **Bresco v Lonsdale [2020] UKSC 25** confirmed a company in liquidation can refer a dispute to adjudication despite insolvency set-off, so the "book of claims" is now realisable via fast adjudication. ISG's September 2024 collapse is the archetype: **EY was appointed administrator to eight ISG entities on 20 September 2024**, with the group working on public-sector projects worth at least **£1.84bn** and owing its supply chain **more than £700m** (trade creditors of the eight administered firms alone owed £308m), and around 2,200 immediate redundancies; much of that book is still being worked. Concrete play: approach the restructuring teams with a standing "claims book triage and valuation" offer, and reference the ring-fencing conditions courts require to enforce an insolvent claimant's award.
- **Bank and lender monitoring surveyors and development funders:** the founder already owns a bank-monitoring software business, which is the wedge. Monitoring surveyors see cost overruns, programme slippage and drawdown disputes months before they surface publicly. Convert those relationships (and the software's user base) into distressed-project introductions. Development finance lenders and specialist funders are the buyers of "is this project recoverable" advice.
- **Barristers' chambers and direct access:** Keating Chambers, Atkin Chambers, 4 Pump Court, Crown Office Chambers, 39 Essex Chambers, Hardwicke/Gatehouse. Juniors and clerks materially influence expert selection; a boutique that makes good juniors look good gets recommended. Direct access is a route to instruction without a solicitor. Concrete play: targeted "who do you use for delay/quantum" cultivation of clerks and mid-level juniors, plus co-authored talks.
- **In-house legal and commercial directors** of contractors and developers are buyers in their own right (standing appointment) as well as via law-firm panels; sell early-warning retainers directly.
- **Sub-panel and framework routes:** local authority, housing association and NHS legal panels; Crown Commercial Service frameworks; and public-sector disputes/expert framework lots. These are procured, so being framework-listed is a prerequisite for public-sector instruction.
- **Reciprocal referral with adjacent non-competing disciplines:** forensic accountants, fire engineers, structural and geotechnical experts. The "who do you use for delay" reflex is the mechanism; build a mutual-referral compact with a handful of complementary experts.

### Part D. Productised and trojan-horse entry points

- **Free/fixed-fee "final account health check", "claim triage" or "records readiness audit":** converts to instruction and creates a relationship without a competitive tender. Low cost, high conversion.
- **VeriCase 90-day, four-case pilots as the wedge** into law firms and contractors: land the software, then the advisory follows the data. Risk: pilots consume partner time and can commoditise; mitigate by tying each pilot to a named partner sponsor and a defined success metric.
- **Early-warning retainers, standing appointments, framework/call-off retainers** with contractors and housing associations: recurring revenue and first-call rights.
- **Accredited CPD and training to law firms and contractors** as a paid trojan horse: SRA-recognised CPD for solicitors and RICS/CIArb-aligned delay-analysis training for commercial teams. Who accredits: solicitors' CPD is now outcomes-based under the SRA, but firms value structured provider content; RICS and CIArb lend credibility to construction CPD.
- **Publish a proprietary data product** that becomes cited and generates inbound: an annual **UK Adjudication Index** or **Gateway Delay Index** built from the firm's own signal engine (TCC enforcement volumes, ANB nomination trends, BSR Gateway medians). This is the boutique answer to HKA's CRUX and Arcadis's report.
- **Secondment models:** embed a delay analyst in a contractor's commercial team; converts to larger instruction and deepens the account.
- **Single joint expert and tribunal-appointed roles:** get partners onto ANB and institutional expert lists (RICS, CIArb) and market availability to the TCC and arbitral community.
- **"Second opinion"/peer review of another expert's report:** low-friction first instruction that showcases capability without displacing the incumbent immediately.
- **Adjudication defence "rapid response" subscription:** exploit the 7-day referral and 28-day decision timetable; responding parties are acutely time-pressured and pay a premium for an instantly available team. A retained "responding party rapid response" offer is genuinely differentiated.

### Part E. Building Safety Act as a white-space acquisition engine

Find the named parties from: the Developer Remediation Contract signatory list and Responsible Actors Scheme (GOV.UK); the monthly Building Safety Remediation data release and per-developer progress chart; Building Safety Fund and Cladding Safety Scheme data; First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) and Upper Tribunal (Lands Chamber) decisions on Remediation Contribution Orders and Building Liability Orders; and Defective Premises Act 1972 claims under the extended limitation (30 years retrospective, 15 years prospective, via s.135 BSA 2022). Triathlon Homes v Stratford Village is the template naming developer, freeholder associate and management company. Brand and productise **Gateway 2 delay quantum** using BSR's own published medians (the 43-week/12-week gap) as the market-sizing evidence.

### Part F. Non-obvious ways to find live disputes right now

- Expert-witness citations in published TCC/arbitration judgments (who acted, hence who is conflicted and who might switch).
- Adjudication enforcement filings in the TCC list (live disputes with named advisers).
- Adjudicator nomination volumes and ANB panel data: **RICS DRS received 1,340 adjudication referrals between May 2023 and April 2024** and states it holds around 60% of the UK market share for appointing construction adjudicators; TeCSA runs the Low Value Disputes service (up to £100,000). Rising nomination volumes and King's College London's Construction Adjudication Report are macro-signals of where disputes are concentrating.
- REIT/housebuilder investor presentations and progress reports showing completion dates slipping.
- Retention release dates and collateral-warranty expiry dates (datable cliffs).
- Limitation cliffs: 6-year (simple contract) and 12-year (deed) limitation; the DPA 1972 30-year retrospective window; disputes cluster 12 to 24 months after practical completion. Build a **calendar-driven origination method**: for each watchlisted project, log practical completion, retention release and limitation-expiry dates, and time the approach to the 12-to-24-month post-PC window and the pre-limitation-expiry rush.

### Part G. Positioning and differentiation against HKA, Ankura, Secretariat, Diales, Systech

The majors win through scale, panel presence and cited thought leadership: HKA's CRUX Insight report (Eighth Annual, "From Insight to Foresight", published 5 November 2025: over 2,200 projects across 114 countries with a combined CapEx of **$2.433 trillion** and an average project CapEx of around **$1.25 billion**; grand total of costs claimed **$95.0 billion**; sums in dispute averaging **33.4%** of contract budgets; time extensions sought averaging **65.8%** of planned schedules); Arcadis's Global Construction Disputes Report (15th Annual, 2025); and the Driver Trett Digest (Diales, hosted at diales.com). These reports feed pipeline by making the firm the reference point.

The boutique's exploitable gaps:
- **Conflicts:** the majors are conflicted out of a large share of instructions because they act for so many parties; a clean-conflict boutique is the natural overflow, if visible at the conflict moment. Make conflict-clearance speed a marketed feature to solicitors and funders.
- **Speed and partner-led delivery:** no junior-heavy leverage model; the partner who pitches does the work. This is decisive on the 28-day adjudication clock.
- **Price and a sharper proprietary publication:** a tightly-scoped UK Adjudication/Gateway Delay Index out-specialises CRUX's global generality for the UK buyer.

## Caveats
- **Find Case Law:** bulk/programmatic analysis is prohibited under the Open Justice Licence and requires a granted computational-analysis licence; do not scrape it before that is in place.
- **Commercial data terms:** The Gazette Data Service, Glenigan and Barbour ABI restrict redistribution; use internally, do not resell.
- **Payment Practices** has no API; scraping must respect the service's terms.
- Several BSR Gateway statistics come from secondary trade-press summaries of BSR releases and vary by 12-week window and category; cite the primary GOV.UK release for any client-facing figure, and note that a single "Gateway 2 processing time" figure is misleading because it depends on category (new-build, remediation, conversion) and reporting window.
- BSF/CSS **building-level naming** is partial; the monthly release leads with aggregates and a dashboard rather than a full public building-by-building register, so verify the exact downloadable files before promising line-level developer/freeholder data.
- Litigation-funding enforceability in England is unsettled post-PACCAR and Therium v Bugsby; pitch funders on decision-quality, not case volume.
- Glenigan/Barbour price bands are indicative third-party figures, not official published rates; both quote on application.
- The Arcadis global 2025 headline value/length figures should be confirmed inside the 15th Annual PDF; the widely-cited UK average of ~£27m over ~14 months is from the 2024 (14th) edition, and the firmly-sourced 2025 figures are the US/North America averages ($60.1m value, ~12.5 months).
- Companies House small-company accounts rarely show a P&L until ECCTA reforms bite, limiting SME financial-signal quality; treat absent data on developer SPVs as a flag rather than reassurance.
