# Research claims audit

Date: 12 September 2026
Source: `docs/compass_artifact_wf-940e6c99-1917-5535-9ade-03216d8ceabf_text_markdown.md`, lines 1–177
Scope: independent review of substantive legal, numerical, market and named-organisation assertions. The whole source document was read. Selected material assertions were checked against accessible primary sources. This is a bounded audit, not confirmation of every source assertion.

## Assessment

The strategy contains useful, verifiable market observations, but requires material corrections before it is used as an evidence-led acquisition plan. The clearest errors concern Building Liability Order jurisdiction, Balfour Beatty's FY2025 financial results, the chronology of Building Safety Regulator performance data, the RIS3 programme period and the continued availability of DIFC-LCIA for new cases.

Public records can establish an event, an appointment or an investment programme. They do not, without additional evidence, establish an available instruction, recoverable damages, dissatisfaction with an incumbent adviser or the probability of a future dispute. The source frequently moves between those propositions without identifying the inference.

The user has confirmed that QCS owns the software. This audit does not raise a separate entity objection to that ownership or question that supplied premise. Corporate history, software capabilities and existing relationship access were not independently investigated.

## Status and confidence

- **Verified:** the specific assertion is supported by the cited source, within its date and scope.
- **Qualified:** part is supported, but scope, timing, interpretation or current status needs correction.
- **Incorrect:** accessible primary evidence contradicts the assertion.
- **Unsupported:** the supplied document and this bounded search did not establish the assertion. This does not prove it false.
- **Not independently verified:** outside the completed checks; no positive or negative conclusion is expressed.

Confidence is **high** where directly supported by accessible primary text, **moderate** where the available source supports only part of the statement or retrieval is incomplete, and **limited** where only a hypothesis or unresolved secondary assertion remains. All web checks were performed on 12 September 2026. Figures retain source currencies because this is a factual audit, not a commercial valuation; no currency conversion is implied.

## Material findings

### C01. Building Liability Orders are assigned to the wrong court

**Lines:** 63 and 115. **Verdict:** Incorrect. **Confidence:** High.

Building Liability Orders are made by the High Court under section 130 of the Building Safety Act 2022. Remediation Contribution Orders are made by the First-tier Tribunal under section 124, with an appellate route. The source incorrectly places both remedies within the FTT/Upper Tribunal system.

**Correction:** distinguish the two remedies, their jurisdictions and their source datasets. Do not describe an FTT remediation order as a High Court Building Liability Order.

**Sources:** [Government redress guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/redress-measures-information-sheet); [Government RCO guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/remediation-contribution-orders/chapter-2-key-definitions); [Building Safety Act explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/30/notes/division/11/index.htm).

### C02. Balfour Beatty's FY2025 results are materially misstated

**Lines:** 70 and 165. **Verdict:** Incorrect. **Confidence:** High.

Balfour Beatty reported a £25 million net non-underlying credit after tax for FY2025, compared with a £49 million charge for FY2024. The source instead states £70 million of charges. Significant FY2025 items included a £49 million pre-tax credit following the NTTA settlement, a £37 million increase in BSA provisions and a £23 million Omnicom disposal gain. The adverse NTTA verdict provision arose in 2024 and was substantially reversed in 2025. The source's £80 million insurance recovery and £30 million warranty release do not match the reviewed FY2025 release.

**Correction:** replace the numerical account and distinguish a live provision, a historic dispute and a settlement credit. The company remains a possible subject for monitoring, but the stated financial premise is unreliable.

**Source:** [Balfour Beatty FY2025 results, printed page 8 and Note 9](https://www.balfourbeatty.com/media/h5kfn0a5/2025-full-year-results-announcement.pdf).

### C06. Regulatory processing delay is not itself compensable delay

**Lines:** 14, 56 and 57. **Verdict:** Qualified. **Confidence:** High for the timetable distinction; case-specific for entitlement.

The statutory determination period is generally 12 weeks for new higher-risk buildings and eight weeks for work to existing higher-risk buildings. Longer periods may be agreed. A statistical excess does not itself establish a compensable delay between employer and contractor.

**Correction:** present Gateway delay work as a potential service requiring review of the contract, responsibility for submissions, compliance, causation, critical-path effect and evidenced loss. Do not translate median weeks into automatic recoverable prolongation costs.

**Source:** [NHS official Gateway guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/prn01337-nhs-estates-technical-bulletin-2024-2.pdf). The entitlement qualification is this audit's legal and analytical assessment, not a finding by BSR about any individual contract.

### C07. Bresco establishes adjudication rights, not automatic recovery

**Lines:** 5, 94 and 142. **Verdict:** Qualified. **Confidence:** High for Bresco; detailed subsequent enforcement conditions not fully reverified.

*Bresco* confirms that a company in liquidation may adjudicate notwithstanding insolvency set-off. The Supreme Court expressly recognises that summary enforcement may be refused. It does not establish that a claims portfolio is readily realisable through fast adjudication.

**Correction:** distinguish entitlement to adjudicate, determination of the net account, enforcement, adequate security and commercial recovery. Refer to a case-specific recovery assessment instead of describing insolvency claims as newly realisable by default.

**Sources:** [Supreme Court Bresco press summary](https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/docs/uksc-2019-0036-press-summary.pdf); [Supreme Court case page](https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2019-0036); [Judiciary record for John Doyle v Erith](https://www.judiciary.uk/judgments/john-doyle-construction-limited-in-liquidation-v-erith-contractors-limited/).

**Retrieval limit:** BAILII blocked retrieval of the full *John Doyle* judgment. Its citation, [2021] EWCA Civ 1452, was confirmed through the Judiciary page. Detailed enforcement requirements should be checked against an accessible judgment before client-facing legal drafting.

### C08. Limitation needs cause-of-action and accrual analysis

**Lines:** 6, 115 and 124. **Verdict:** Qualified. **Confidence:** High for the statutory distinction; no individual expiry date verified.

The 30-year retrospective and 15-year prospective headline is correct for DPA section 1, subject to commencement and accrual rules. The new section 2A duty concerning work to existing dwellings is prospective. A generic 30-year period cannot be applied to all BSA remedies.

**Correction:** record the relevant duty and cause of action, accrual date, execution form and any applicable contractual provisions before calculating an expiry. Practical completion alone is not a reliable universal limitation start date. Do not infer that warranty expiry, a defects period and statutory limitation are the same event.

**Source:** [Building Safety Act explanatory notes, section 135](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/30/notes/division/11/index.htm).

### C24. Predictive strength and competitive exclusivity are unvalidated

**Lines:** 4–6, 10, 12, 14, 16, 26–30, 36, 41, 43, 57, 71, 74, 88, 93, 103, 111, 119, 124, 130–133 and 155. **Verdict:** Unsupported as general factual assertions. **Confidence:** Limited.

**Correction:** label these as hypotheses. Distinguish observed event, inferred need, verified buyer, accessible route and instruction outcome. Validate the proposed scores against dated outcomes and avoid treating two correlated signals as independent evidence. No primary evidence was verified for the stronger predictive or universal competitive assertions.
